Each row on the left is one source line that pside slowed the rest of the program around, at twenty-one virtual speedups from 0% to 100%. The trace is what the program's throughput did in response, measured against that line's own baseline.

Amber is above that baseline, cobalt is below. The wall shows medians only, because a 95% interval has no room to be read at row height. Open a line to see how wide its evidence actually is.

How to read this

virtual speedup

pside cannot make a line faster, so it slows every other thread by the same amount whenever the line runs. Level n is a 5n% virtual speedup of that line. If the program's throughput climbs with the level, the line is on the critical path; if it stays flat, making the line faster buys nothing.

peak Δ

The largest median on the ladder, minus the median at level 0. It is one scan for the highest point. The interval plays no part in it, and neither does the shape on the way there. Rank by it to surface lines that moved, rather than lines that carry throughput.

avg floor

pside solves each level's median distribution in closed form over that level's own order statistics and keeps the middle 95%. The bounds are exact, and identical on every run of the report.

This figure averages the low end of that range across the whole ladder. It is a throughput in the units on the axis, so it reads straight against the baseline and against any other line: the outcome your samples support, rather than the one they hint at.

what it will not tell you

Samples from the same run move together and pside can no longer tell them apart, so the interval is a floor on the true width, not the width. A bias that hit every measurement equally is invisible to it. And the ladder is a ranking, not a verdict: a trace that leaves its own interval is not on its own evidence that the line matters.

what is missing

A line needs at least five samples at every one of the twenty-one levels to appear here at all. Lines below that gate were dropped when the profile was loaded, so this list is the measured subset, not the whole program.
